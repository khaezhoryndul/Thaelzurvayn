# Thaelzurvayn

> Beneath an iron blossom, a sleeping voice wears the shape of a key.

## A Tree That Flowers in Rust

The tree grows in the center of an empty theater. Its roots pass beneath the seats, and its branches reach the painted sky above the stage. Each spring it produces iron blossoms. Their petals open without a sound, revealing small hollows lined with red velvet.

No audience has attended the theater within living memory. Nevertheless, the seats are warm every evening, and a program lies folded on each one. The programs contain a single sentence written in a language that becomes unfamiliar as soon as it is understood.

The caretaker gathers the programs before dawn. She binds them with white string and stores them in the orchestra pit. By noon, they have become feathers. She never looks down while making this discovery, but knows by the change in their weight.

## The Seat That Was Reserved

One seat remains cold. It stands at the end of the seventh row and bears a brass plate without a number. Its program is printed on grey silk. Its folded edges are sealed with wax bearing the impression of a closed eye.

A visitor came to claim this seat. He wore a mask shaped like his own face and carried a ticket cut from thin bark. The caretaker examined it beneath a blossom. Inside the velvet hollow, something stirred.

She asked whether he had come to listen or to be heard. He said he had come to recover a voice. She returned the ticket and showed him to the seventh row.

When he sat down, the cold seat became warm. Around him, every other seat cooled. In the painted sky above the stage, one cloud began moving against the direction of the painted wind.

## A Performance Below the Floor

The curtain rose without revealing a stage. Behind it lay a staircase descending into a room full of water. Chairs stood on its ceiling. A chandelier rested on the floor, its candles burning downward into the dark.

At the center of the room, a woman sat with both hands over her mouth. A key hung from her wrist. Each time she moved, the key struck a bracelet and produced a note that the visitor recognized from a song he had never finished.

The caretaker remained beside him. She said the woman had been waiting since the tree's first blossom. Every year a new petal formed around her voice, and every year the distance between the room and the theater grew shorter.

The visitor removed his mask. Beneath it, his face looked younger, but the mask continued watching the staircase with eyes that had never been painted.

## The Creature Among the Seats

The feathers in the orchestra pit began to rise. They arranged themselves around a shape too large to fit beneath the stage. A long neck emerged between the rows. Its scales were dark iron; its breath smelled of rain falling on warm leaves.

The creature's antlered head turned toward the reserved seat. One eye remained closed. In the other, the visitor saw the theater filled with people whose faces were hidden behind programs.

Its wings extended beneath the roots. Every iron blossom opened wider. The woman below the floor lowered one hand from her mouth, and the key on her wrist turned slowly without touching a lock.

The visitor tried to speak. His voice emerged from the grey silk program instead. The sealed wax softened. The impression of the closed eye became a narrow doorway.

## The Sentence on the Ticket

The bark ticket had acquired writing along its edge:

*Thael — zur — vayn — oshen.*

He read the words silently. The caretaker heard them and stepped away from the seat. Beneath the floor, the woman lowered her other hand. Her mouth held a small iron blossom, its petals closed tightly around something bright.

The key fell from her wrist and drifted upward through the water. It passed through the stage, crossed the theater, and came to rest inside the visitor's mask. The mask closed its eyes.

The creature folded its wings. Its feathers settled into the orchestra pit. Above the stage, the moving cloud stopped, leaving a thin opening in the painted sky through which real darkness could be seen.

## When the Curtain Fell

The visitor left the mask on the reserved seat. He carried the grey program into the street, where the silk became a feather in his hand. For the first time in many years, he could remember the ending of his song.

He did not sing it. A warmth beneath his tongue told him that the iron blossom had opened somewhere else.

Inside the theater, the caretaker gathered the programs. The seventh seat was cold again. On it lay the mask, facing upward, and inside its mouth a key shaped like a sleeping voice.

The tree continues to flower each spring.

The orchestra pit continues to fill with feathers.

And when rain touches the roof, the empty theater listens with every seat but one.
